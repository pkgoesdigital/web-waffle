## Safety Incidents

### Trespasser on Porch, Attempted Entry (Oct 6, early morning)
A man was found sleeping on a resident's porch. He was responsive but refused to leave. The resident called police and, after more than 40 minutes with only automated text updates, called for paramedics. Fire/EMS arrived first and woke him, but he lay back down. An officer then arrived and reviewed the home's doorbell footage, which showed the man trying to open the front door at about 2:15am, and arrested him. Neighbors circulated a description so they could watch for him after release.

## Resident Observations and Themes

From the WhatsApp "High and Race St Safety" group (Oct 1–31, paraphrased). October was noticeably quieter in the chat than August and September:

- **Police response times, not response itself, are the October issue.** In the Oct 6 porch trespass, the resident waited more than 40 minutes with only automated texts, and Fire/EMS arrived before police. Residents pointed out how many costly resources were used on what should have been a quick trespass removal. Officers did make the arrest and responded to the Oct 23 noise complaint, but after long waits.
- **Residents are sharing evidence.** Doorbell footage was decisive in the Oct 6 arrest, which reinforces the value of the camera and lighting hardening residents have been adding.
- **Calling in together works.** For the Oct 23 noise complaint, multiple neighbors called non-emergency, and dispatch confirmed others had already reported it before sending an officer.
- **Positive reaction to enforcement:** residents welcomed news of DPD and DEA's Operation Overdose Intercept results (reported as 107 arrests and 223 pounds of drugs seized) and asked for more of the same — the same initiative District 2 promoted in its Sept 22 and Oct 7 emails.
- **Activity has shifted from crime to quality of life.** October reports were fewer and lower-severity (noise, stolen and moved trash cans, a discarded alarm, continued concern about invasive "tree of heaven" growth in the alleys) than in August and September. That may reflect the colder weather or the TGP and DPD efforts.
- **DPD was absent from the Oct 6 GNC meeting** — the same morning as the porch arrest. Community Resource Officers have been standing GNC members, and their absence came at a time when TGP's alcove gate, lighting, and landscaping commitments are still unresolved. The Sinclair noise incident was across from TGP, the area the GNC is focused on.
- **TGP's physical-security fixes keep stalling:** the alcove gate has been delayed by funding for several months, solar light installation is unconfirmed, and the problem tree remains. Staff turnover at TGP (Jake's departure) may slow follow-through.
- **DPD's citywide messaging is steady but repetitive:** the October bulletins repeated September's list, and no new CPW-specific items were issued.
- **The watch group is maturing.** New members joined, including a resident near 16th & Gilpin. The group has an established communication channel, an online presence, and RNO support, and is now seeking formal recognition and materials from DPD.
