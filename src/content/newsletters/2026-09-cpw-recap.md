## Safety Incidents

### Shooting — 1900 block of E Colfax / 7-Eleven (Sept 5, ~2:30am)
Residents heard about seven shots at roughly 2:30am, and police were swarming the location by about 2:45am. The incident also showed up in Nextdoor safety alerts. That block runs from High to Race, so this is very likely the same incident. *(WhatsApp and the DPD bulletin overlap.)*

## Resident Observations and Themes

From the WhatsApp "High and Race St Safety" group (Sept 1–30, paraphrased):

- **Frustration is high.** Several residents described the neighborhood as chaotic, and one long-time resident is considering selling. Others credited the watch group and DPD engagement with real, if slow, progress, and urged everyone to keep reporting.
- **911 vs. non-emergency is still unclear.** Residents struggle with the 311/non-emergency phone prompts, and some believed DPD leadership had said open drug use can't be acted on. Watch leaders corrected this: crimes in progress, including open drug use, go to 911. Online non-emergency reports with photos have typically brought a patrol within a few hours.
- **911 still works when non-emergency doesn't.** On Sept 8, neighbors who couldn't get through on 311 or non-emergency called 911 and got a response. Watch leaders reiterated that a disturbance like that is a 911 call.
- **Frustration with the "crime in progress" threshold:** residents want clearer guidance on when prowling or loitering warrants a response, and when to request STAR instead. Behavioral-health calls keep recurring.
- **The 7-Eleven / 1900 block of Colfax is the month's focal point.** There was a confirmed shooting, frequent police presence, activity displaced into surrounding streets and alleys, and a nearby resident in repeated crisis. After the shooting, residents asked about a Good Neighbor Agreement or a Nuisance Abatement complaint against the 7-Eleven, pointing to the Ogden St 7-Eleven, which closed — that area has felt safer since. Prior outreach to the business has gone unanswered.
- **DPD's citywide focus lines up with what residents are seeing:** the Violence Suppression Plan, Operation Overdose Intercept, and the Colfax drug and firearm arrest in the Officer of the Month nominations all match resident concerns about open drug use and gunfire on Colfax.
- **Open drug use near children** (Illegal Pete's lot by the school) is escalating resident concern.
- **The Race/High alley remains the recurring hot spot.** The second half of the month was quieter on WhatsApp, and reports shifted from Colfax toward the alley: an encampment forming, suspected bike theft, and dumped trash — the same corridor flagged in July and August.
- **Some improvement at Colfax & High.** After Paula shared the TGP Good Neighbor Committee agenda (Sept 6), one resident said they've noticed a change for the better at that corner — an early sign the TGP and DPD efforts are working. At the same time, Good Neighbor Committee momentum stalled as the Sept 9 meeting slipped to Oct 6.
- **Residents are hardening properties and doing their own outreach.** A watch member installed a floodlight camera across from TGP, following DPD's recommendation to add deterrents around TGP. Another posted a safety-resources board in their building's laundry room, and interest in another flyering round is growing.
- **Communication friction:** a wrong phone number on CRO materials, inconsistent numbers across the card and brochure, and a broken email link for Officer C. in DPD's own emails.
- **WhatsApp admin access:** members say anyone can add people to the group, but Paula is blocked by a permissions setting, so onboarding new neighbors is still stuck.
